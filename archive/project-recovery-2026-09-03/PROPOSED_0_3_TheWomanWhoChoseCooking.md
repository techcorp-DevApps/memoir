# THE WOMAN WHO CHOSE COOKING

I kept my head down. Read when I could. Worked in the kitchen—ironic, in hindsight—peeling potatoes, washing dishes, staying out of everyone's way.

Got released early. Good behaviour.

But the conditions were crippling.

---

At first, it was daily reporting. Show up in person. Every single day. Pop my head in the door, wave, prove I wasn't drunk or high, leave.

My first parole officer was this old Māori bloke. Absolute good cunt. Didn't hassle you as long as you were on time and sober. Some days I didn't even have to go all the way to his office. Just stuck my head in, waved, signed off.

Then he retired.

And everything went to shit.

---

Her name was Sarah. Or maybe Sharon. I honestly can't remember, which tells you how much I wanted to forget those meetings.

She was young. Late twenties. Fresh out of whatever degree you need to become a parole officer. By the book in a way that wasn't cruel—just relentless.

*This is the policy and I will follow it to the letter.*

Somehow worse than cruelty. You can't argue with policy.

The first time I met her, I was ten minutes late.

"You're late."

"Yeah, bus was—"

"I don't need to hear it. Sit down."

Her office was small. Beige. Stacked with files and a dying pot plant on the windowsill. Photo of a golden retriever on her desk.

Everything about the room said: *I care very much about rules and also dogs.*

She flipped open my file.

"High risk of reoffending."

"Yep."

"You've breached every bail condition."

"Yep."

"You've been arrested how many times?"

"Lost count."

She looked up at me. Not unkindly. Not warmly either. Just assessing.

"You need a job."

"I'm looking."

"You need structure."

"Yeah."

"You need to be somewhere every day where people are watching you."

She picked up the phone. Dialled. I sat there while she spoke to someone on the other end.

> "Hi, yes, I'm calling about your courses. What's starting in the next week?"

Long pause. Scribbling on a notepad.

> "Commercial cookery? Level two? When does that start?"

Another pause.

> "Perfect. I'm enrolling someone. I'll send the paperwork today."

She hung up. Looked at me.

"You're starting a cooking course on Monday."

"What?"

"Commercial cookery. Level two. Polytech. Every day, nine to five. Miss a day, I'll breach you, and you'll go back to finish your sentence. Understand?"

I stared at her.

"I don't want to be a fucking cook."

"I don't care what you want." Not meanly. Just factually. "You need structure, supervision, and a skillset. This is what's available. You start Monday. Nine a.m."

"What if I want to do something else?"

"Like what?"

I didn't have an answer.

"That's what I thought."
