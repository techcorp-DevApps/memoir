# THE CLOWN SUIT

I was up at 0630.

Prison schedule. The body remembers.

I shaved my head—did that most mornings—trimmed my goatee, put on the clothes my mum had bought me the day before. Black steel-cap boots, polished, 12-up laced. Dress pants—not business pants, not slacks, somewhere in between. Collared polo shirt. All black.

Mum and I didn't have the best relationship back then. Still don't, really. But she'd taken me shopping. Paid for everything. Didn't say much. Just handed over her card when I picked out what I needed.

A gesture of intent, I suppose.

I rode my BMX to polytech. Full panic mode by the time I got there. Not because I was scared of the course—I wasn't scared of anything like that. But I wasn't a civilian. Not by any definition. Hadn't been since I left school at thirteen. I had nothing in common with anyone in that building.

Nothing.

I parked my bike against the wall outside the classroom. Didn't lock it. Didn't need to. Who the fuck would be crazy enough to steal my bike?

That was the mindset.

Rolled a cigarette. Had a smoke. Definitely nervous about the environment—completely out of my comfort zone. But confident in myself. I always was.

I could carry my own. No problem.

---

I finished the smoke. Walked in.

The classroom was theory only. About twenty students—different ages, different backgrounds. School leavers through to people in their mid-thirties. Everyone talking amongst themselves.

Then I walked in.

Everyone stopped.

Looked at me.

I was definitely out of place in that room.

Then they went back to talking. A little more quietly.

The tutor was mid-forties. His name was Glen. Wool cardigan. Slacks. Leather business shoes. The kind of attire I would've laughed at in another life.

But I had respect for others by default. Whether you kept that respect was your choice—and it was decided quickly based on how you conducted yourself.

Strange thing about life in criminal circles. There's still a moral code of sorts.

I walked straight up to Glen and shook his hand. Introduced myself.

Glen nodded. Shook my hand. Didn't seem nervous.

"Take a seat wherever you like."

I found a spot at the back. On my own.

---

The morning passed.

Theory. Introduction to the course. What we'd be covering. The format—theory, theory, practical, theory test, practical test. Repeat.

Some of the younger ones kept talking during the session.

I lost my shit.

"Show some fucken respect when someone's trying to teach us something. Other people actually want to learn."

The room went quiet.

One of the lads had a little giggle.

Which sparked me up more.

"Something funny, mate? Want to step outside with me?"

The kid went bright red. Shrank back in his seat.

That's when Glen intervened. Carefully. Not backing down, but not escalating either.

> "I appreciate the sentiment. But that's not how we handle things in a classroom environment."

I understood.

I respected it.

Didn't mean I liked it.

But it was no different to jail, really. There's a time and a place, and this wasn't it. And at the end of the day, I was trying to achieve the same goal as Glen—people paying attention, showing respect. Undermining him would've been contradictory.

So I nodded.

Sat back down.

The kid stayed inside during the first break. Didn't come out with everyone else.

Fair enough.

---

The practical sessions started a few days later.

I hated the uniform.

Little hat. All whites. Those blue-and-white checked pants—looked like something a clown would wear. I felt like a fucking idiot.

But the knives.

The knives were something else.

Brand new Wüsthof set. Fucking choice. The weight of them. The shine. The way they sat in the roll like they were waiting for something.

I took them home that night to show Stacey.

Laid them out on the kitchen table like they were jewellery. Showed her each one. The chef's knife. The paring knife. The boning knife. All of it.

She smiled. Didn't say much. Just let me show her.

That was the first thing I'd taken home in a long time that didn't need to be hidden or explained away.

---

The first practical unit was salads, sandwiches, dressings, and roasting.

We made focaccia from scratch. Made a mayo. Made a salad. Roasted a little ham. Then assembled a sandwich.

I didn't know what the fuck I was doing with the bread.

I knew how to cook—I'd been out of home for a long time, always fairly poor, but I could feed myself. Roasts. Bolognese. Even the odd batch of cookies. Basic survival stuff.

But focaccia? Dough? Proving? Technique?

That was different.

I was in my own zone, though. The other students disappeared. It was just me and the bench and the task in front of me. I wasn't going to ask for help. I'd figure it out. That's what I'd always done.

I only just finished in time.

But I did it.

Took a big ham salad sandwich home for Stacey that night.

Fucking choice.

---

Stacey had been with me for several years by then.

She knew my life up until that point. All of it. And it didn't seem to faze her much, despite being brought up by her grandparents with her three sisters in a very strict household. The opposite of everything I was.

I told her she was nuts when she said she wanted to move in together.

I had nothing.

Less than nothing.

She did it anyway.

---

I found the course challenging.

The whole way through.

Not just the first week or the first month—the entire course. Every unit. Every practical. Every exam. It didn't come naturally to me the way some things did. I had to grind for it.

But I did.

By the end of the second year, I'd made it into the top percentile. To get invited into the final year, you had to score in the top bracket.

I did.

During the final year, I was also invited to sit the City and Guilds exam.

Passed with distinction.

For the first time, I started to think:

*Maybe this is actually going somewhere.*
