# GRANT'S KITCHEN

I finished the course and started looking for work.

Given my location—bottom of the South Island—there wasn't much. So I started looking in Auckland. I'd lived there before. Knew the city.

Found a job listing on Seek. Applied just like any normal person would have to.

*Just like any normal person.*

That phrase meant something. Applying through proper channels. Waiting for a callback. Going through an interview. The kind of thing people do every day without thinking about it. For me, it was new territory.

They were looking for a junior level chef. With my qualifications and the City and Guilds distinction, I fit the requirements well.

No need to disclose anything prior.

Which worked out just fine.

---

The restaurant was small. Maybe fifty seats. Usually just a single sitting.

There was Grant—the head chef—me, and a kitchen hand. On Fridays and Saturdays we'd have an additional hand to help with cold sides.

Grant was an older guy. Relaxed. Pretty sure he was a high-functioning alcoholic—something I later learned to be fairly normal in the industry. He held it together well enough, and the food wasn't all that technical. Basic stuff, done well.

The pace was the thing.

In culinary school, you focus on one dish until it's done. In an actual service, you're running three tickets at once and they all need to land at the same time.

That took getting used to.

---

About six months in, Grant pulled me aside.

> "I've got a feeling the owners are going to sell. It's on the cards."

He said it calmly. Like he was telling me the weather.

> "I'm going to take some leave before that happens. I want to get you ready to run service while I'm gone."

He brought in a temp—someone he knew well, someone who'd be a good help during that stretch—and that's what happened.

I ran that kitchen.

Even small. Even basic food. Even with help.

I ran it.

And it went fine. Better than fine. I'd thought I could do it before Grant told me I could. Him confirming it—him trusting me enough to leave—that meant something.

When he came back, he confirmed the sale was going through.

> "Start looking now. Don't wait until they make the announcement. You don't want to be caught in the cold."

So I started looking.

Found a listing for a vineyard restaurant in New Zealand.

Applied.

Got the call.
