# Context Profile Assessment
The chapter is narrated in-voice for most of its length — blunt, specific, unsentimental (the Marco cut/"Finally. Some actual fucking flavour from your section" exchange, lines 19–29, is exactly the dark-humour-as-survival mechanic the profile calls for, §7.1). The narrator's own emotional register stays largely implicit through action (freezing at Sam's approach, waiting for "what are you doing," lines 55–71) rather than declared.

The exception is the closing third. Lines 115–147 shift from scene into direct thesis-statement mode: "I think about why we stay" ... "That's why we stay. / Not for the food. Not for the money... / For the moments when someone who's seen your worst decides you're worth keeping." This is the narrator explaining the chapter's meaning to the reader rather than trusting the preceding scenes (which had already done the work) to land it. It is in-voice phrasing, not sentimental or soft — but it is the profile's own "force profundity" / "explain the meaning of every scene" violation (§12), applied to a chapter that didn't need it.

# Voice Standard Assessment
**Introduced defect, line 33:** "That's when I realised: this isn't cruelty. This is survival." This is a direct instance of the exact construction CLAUDE.md's Hard "No" List names — "Forced epiphanies or tidy lessons" — worded almost identically to the pattern flagged in Batch 2's `13_HotTrays.md` ("I realised something important: In the kitchen, nothing warns you before it hurts you"). The Marco scene that precedes it (lines 19–32) already renders the point without needing the line — the brigade's laughter and Marco's own participation in it is the "showing." The sentence after it is redundant explanation.

**Second instance, lines 143–147:** the doubled "That's why we stay" is not a stated-emotion line in the "I felt X" sense, but it is the same over-explaining reflex — stating the chapter's thesis outright rather than trusting the "You're starting to look like you belong here" beat (lines 127–139), which is genuinely excellent restrained writing and did not need the paragraph that follows it to work.

By contrast, the Sam "Go smoke" sequence (lines 53–89) is the chapter's strongest passage and shows no such defect — it ends on "But I'd take a bullet for that man," which is a stated line but is earned, specific, and consistent with the established Fat Sam arc (matches CLAUDE.md § 5's "Go smoke. I've got this." almost verbatim).
