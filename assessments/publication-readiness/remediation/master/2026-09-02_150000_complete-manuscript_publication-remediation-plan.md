### REM-001 — Ch.11 generic pop-culture comparison + unanchored strawman
- **Severity**: MEDIUM · **Priority**: P2 · **Category**: B
- **Chapter(s)**: 11 (`chapters/11_TheOnion.md`)
- **Source(s)**: ch.11 assessment §Voice Standard Assessment; synthesis §5
- **Issue**: "this sexy, Gordon Ramsay-lit fantasy" leans on the TV/foodie-fantasy trope the voice profile explicitly says the narrator rejects (§3: "Loathes... MasterChef-style delusions"). The "Could you, Darren? Could you really?" bit invents an unnamed strawman not grounded in any lived scene, unlike the manuscript's other humour (always tied to a named person or real incident).
- **Evidence**: `chapters/11_TheOnion.md` — cited lines per ch.11 assessment's Voice Standard Assessment section.
- **Root cause**: Isolated register drift during drafting of the 11–13 ChefTip block — not a pattern that recurs before or after this three-chapter stretch (see REM-001–009 shared root-cause note below).
- **Required outcome**: Chapter reads in the same register as 09/10/15/18 — no TV-trope comparison used as the narrator's own descriptive frame; the humour beat is anchored to a specific person or incident.
- **Remediation strategy**: Cut or rewrite the "Gordon Ramsay-lit fantasy" clause — replace with a comparison drawn from the narrator's own lived kitchen vocabulary (matches the pattern used everywhere else: brine physics in ch.21, chopping-board towel in ch.09). Replace "Darren" with either a named, previously-established minor character reacting to a real remembered moment, or cut the bit entirely if no such anchor exists — do not invent a new named character solely to patch this line (see Preservation Constraints, §8).
- **Preservation constraints**: Keep the ChefTip #10 rule text ("Square your damn ingredients before you chop. Always.") and the hook→scene→escalation→tip structure exactly as is. Keep the chapter's comic energy and profanity register — the fix is anchoring, not softening.
- **Dependencies**: None blocking; sequence with REM-002–009 as one voice pass (Batch 2).
- **Governing impact**: None — the voice profile is accurate and current; the chapter departs from it.
- **Validation method**: Re-run the chapter through the voice-profile Implementation Checklist (profile §13); confirm no TV/pop-culture framing remains and the new anchor is a specific, previously-real detail, not an invention.

### REM-002 — Ch.12 rendering-vs-stating violations (two instances)
- **Severity**: HIGH · **Priority**: P2 · **Category**: B
- **Chapter(s)**: 12 (`chapters/12_TheStockPot.md`)
- **Source(s)**: ch.12 assessment §Voice Standard Assessment (direct citation of CLAUDE.md Hard "No" List)
- **Issue**: "internally acknowledging that I am absolutely not built for the level of responsibility that pot required" and "Feeling almost... capable?" both name the feeling directly rather than rendering it through action/physical detail — a direct instance of the Hard "No" List's "Stating emotions instead of rendering them."
- **Evidence**: `chapters/12_TheStockPot.md`, lines cited in the ch.12 assessment.

Rewrite line 2 to show a concrete action or sensory beat that implies growing competence, dropping the "Feeling almost... capable?" construction entirely — the hedge does not rescue a stated-emotion line.
- **Preservation constraints**: Keep the chapter's comic self-deprecation and the ChefTip #9 rule text ("Never turn your back on a stockpot...") intact. Do not turn the rewritten beats somber or lose the humour — render through action, not through gravity.
- **Dependencies**: Sequence with REM-003 (same chapter, same pass).
- **Governing impact**: None — governing files are accurate; chapter departs from them.
- **Validation method**: Confirm neither rewritten passage contains a bare "I felt X" / "I was Y" construction; confirm a beta reader (or Bosco) can identify the emotion from the action alone before reading the surrounding prose.

## 4. Root-cause analysis

Five root causes explain the great majority of the 45 REM items:

1. **A single contained drafting-pass register drift (chapters 11–14).** REM-001 through REM-009 all trace to one drafting pass that produced three consecutive ChefTip essays plus one Kitchen Camaraderie essay in a noticeably more generic, more explanatory register than the chapters immediately before and after it. This is not a manuscript-wide voice problem — the audit confirmed no recurrence in 15 chapters before it or 7 after — and should be remediated as one bounded voice pass (Batch 2), not as four unrelated chapter problems.

2. **Reference tables lagging finished prose.** REM-032, REM-033, REM-035, REM-041, REM-016 (in part) all trace to the same structural pattern: `CLAUDE.md` §5's character tables are updated less often than new chapters are drafted. This is a process gap, not a manuscript defect, and the fix is procedural (update the table whenever a chapter introduces a new named character) as much as it is a one-time catch-up edit.

3. **Governing-file drift through two title changes and one major structural ruling.** REM-037, REM-039, REM-045, REM-047, and half of REM-031 all trace to the 30 Aug 2026 retitling and B30 structural ruling not fully propagating through every governing file. `CLAUDE.md` itself was updated; several files it references (`context/chef-writer-context-profile.md`, `context/project-memory.md`) were not.

4. **An external reference file (`canon.md`) that this repository cannot act on.** REM-036 is its own root cause: several findings the original audit treated as "ready to action" (Ivian's gender, the fine-dining-covers figure) are actually blocked because their home file doesn't exist here. This plan resolves the blockage by moving the two settled facts into `CLAUDE.md` directly rather than waiting on a file this repo doesn't control.

5. **Unwritten future content, correctly out of scope for a remediation pass.** REM-026, REM-027, REM-042, REM-043 are not defects — they are known, already-ruled gaps in not-yet-written material. They appear in this register only because the task specification requires every finding to have a recorded disposition; their disposition is "no remediation action, tracked for future drafting scope."

Two findings are genuinely singular and don't fit these clusters: REM-021 (the B30 foreshadowing tension, which requires Bosco's personal judgment about his own family history) and REM-020 (the ch.03 dual-H1 structure, a deferred authorial decision, not an error).
