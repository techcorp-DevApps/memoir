# Perko — Future Arc Development Record

## Purpose

Preserve newly recovered factual context about Perko and identify the potential for a later post-release character arc without contaminating the chronology or split-temporal perspective of **FOUR FLIGHTS**.

This file is a planning and continuity record only.

It does **not** authorise:
- changes to the current FOUR FLIGHTS publication-review candidate;
- disclosure of later-known information inside the incarceration timeline;
- assignment of a future chapter number or title;
- promotion of this material into `chapters/`;
- invention of dialogue, motives, sensory detail, dates, or connecting events.

Any later manuscript development must be reconstructed from the author's recollection in chronological context.

---

## 1. Perko as known during incarceration

During incarceration, Perko did **not** openly disclose that he was a young Crip / Cripster.

The narrator did not know about that affiliation while they were inside.

Nothing in **FOUR FLIGHTS** should retroactively reveal, foreshadow, hint at, or explain the affiliation. Doing so would give the younger narrator knowledge he did not possess at the time and would break the established split-temporal / dual-consciousness rule.

What the narrator did come to know during their time together in general population was Perko himself.

Established recollection:
- bright energy;
- a great sense of humour;
- kind;
- easy to spend time with;
- did not present as what the younger narrator would then have considered a stereotypical gangster;
- the narrator became aware of these qualities through ordinary time together in general population.

This is the Perko available to the incarceration-era narrator.

If FOUR FLIGHTS later requires any further Perko character development, it should remain confined to qualities genuinely known inside.

---

## 2. Information learned outside

After release, the narrator became aware that Perko rolled with some seriously hardcore lads on the outside and was connected with a Crip-affiliated crew.

The important continuity point is that this was **later knowledge**.

The difference between the person known inside and the outside-world context discovered later has potential narrative value precisely because it was not visible during incarceration.

The later material should therefore reveal this information in real time rather than explaining it retrospectively in FOUR FLIGHTS.

---

## 3. Post-release house visit — established recollection

At some point after release, the narrator went to visit Perko at his house.

The narrator arrived wearing:
- black boots;
- white laces;
- camouflage pants;
- black singlet;
- shaven head.

The house was down a long driveway.

The narrator's only backup was waiting in the car, out of sight.

When the narrator knocked on the door:
- a Black man answered wearing blue;
- multiple other young adult Black males were visible inside;
- they were also wearing blue;
- the narrator was not initially certain he was even at the correct house.

The combination was immediately recognised as having serious potential for conflict.

The narrator was heavily outnumbered.

### Narrator behaviour and internal calculation

The narrator did not show fear or outward uncertainty.

Confidence remained intact externally.

Internally, however, the situation was assessed immediately:
- numbers;
- colours;
- clothing;
- location;
- long driveway;
- backup out of sight;
- uncertainty about whether this was the correct address.

The author's shorthand for the internal assessment:

> The calculations were done and the math wasn't mathing.

This should not later be rewritten as panic, nor should it be inflated into retrospective bravado.

The useful distinction is between **visible confidence** and **active situational calculation**. They coexisted.

---

## 4. Why nothing happened

Perko had sufficient standing within that crew that the narrator was able to enter and leave the house safely.

Some of the other men were not particularly welcoming.

Despite that atmosphere, no violence occurred.

The narrator's relationship with Perko, and Perko's standing within the group, were sufficient for the visit to proceed without incident.

This is narratively significant because the resolution does not require the narrator to become invulnerable or the surrounding tension to be retrospectively dismissed.

Perko's position is demonstrated by what happened:

the narrator walked in, remained there, and walked back out without incident.

No dialogue around Perko's standing should be invented unless the author later remembers it.

---

## 5. Later outcome

Approximately eighteen months after their release, Perko died by suicide.

The death sent shock waves through several different groups of people.

No further detail about the circumstances of his death is established in this record.

Do not infer:
- motive;
- mental state;
- precipitating event;
- exact date;
- method;
- whether warning signs existed;
- what Perko had disclosed to anyone beforehand.

Those matters require direct author recollection or another verified source before manuscript use.

## 6. Potential later Perko arc

There is now clear potential for Perko to develop beyond his incarceration appearance into a later post-release character arc.

A possible chronological architecture, subject to future author development, is:

### A. Incarceration — the person actually known

Perko appears through general-population experience as:
- funny;
- kind;
- energetic;
- unexpectedly easy company;
- someone whose outside affiliations are not apparent to the narrator.

The reader knows only what the narrator knew.

### B. Post-release — the outside world becomes visible

The house visit reveals that Perko's outside-world relationships are materially different from what the narrator could see inside.

The revelation should happen through scene:
- arrival;
- clothing;
- long driveway;
- door opening;
- blue everywhere;
- immediate internal calculation;
- unwelcoming reactions from some of the men;
- Perko's standing becoming evident through the fact that the narrator can safely enter and leave.

This has potential to deepen both men without requiring explanatory narration about gang hierarchy.

### C. Later loss

Perko's suicide approximately eighteen months after release changes the meaning of the accumulated memories without needing to contaminate the earlier scenes with foreshadowing.

The later death may give the arc substantial emotional consequence, particularly because it affected several otherwise separate groups of people.

The chronology should do the work.

The reader should first know Perko alive.

## 7. Split-temporal rule for this arc

The established project rule applies directly:

- the younger narrator may know only what he genuinely knew at that point;
- the present-day narrator must not leak later knowledge into earlier scenes merely because it is now available;
- hindsight may enter when it adds consequence or understanding unavailable at the time;
- hindsight should not protect the narrator from his younger self or pre-explain another person's eventual fate.

Accordingly:

**FOUR FLIGHTS**
- may contain the Perko experienced in prison;
- must not identify him as a Crip / Cripster;
- must not foreshadow the house scene;
- must not foreshadow his suicide.

**Later post-release material**
- may reveal the affiliation when the narrator discovered it;
- may develop the house encounter;
- may later address Perko's death once the chronology reaches it.

---

## 8. Editorial opportunity

The strength of a future Perko arc is the contrast between contexts rather than a surprise reveal engineered for effect.

Inside:
- bright;
- funny;
- kind;
- no overt gang identity disclosed.

Outside:
- connected to a serious crew;
- enough standing within that group to make the narrator's presence acceptable despite obvious potential friction.

Later:
- his suicide affects multiple groups of people.

These elements should not be collapsed into a character summary.

They have the potential to accumulate across separate scenes and allow the reader's understanding of Perko to expand at roughly the same pace as the narrator's did.
