# Waitane — Incarceration Arc Development Record

## Purpose

Preserve newly recovered factual context about **Waitane**, Perko's celly, and identify the potential for a fuller incarceration-era character arc.

This is a planning and continuity record only.

It does **not** authorise:
- automatic insertion into FOUR FLIGHTS;
- promotion into `chapters/`;
- invention of dialogue, motives, dates, sensory details, or connecting events;
- expansion of the boxing sequence beyond what the author actually remembers.

Any manuscript development remains subject to author review in chronological context.

---

## 1. Identity and relationship

Confirmed spelling: **Waitane**.

Waitane was Perko's celly.

The narrator got to know him well over the course of their incarceration.

Established recollection:
- very quiet;
- strongly introverted;
- no gang affiliation;
- generally avoided confrontation rather than sought it;
- incarcerated for domestic assault;
- half Māori;
- later discovered to be a skilled featherweight boxer and regional champion.

The important character distinction is that none of this boxing capability was obvious from the way Waitane carried himself day to day.

He did not present as aggressive, loud, gang-connected, or eager to prove himself.

---

## 2. Classification / early impression

In the earlier incarceration period, Waitane and Perko were celled together.

The narrator initially knew Waitane as one of the quieter men around him.

His personality did not advertise what he was physically capable of.

That contrast matters.

The later boxing incident works because it reveals something that had been present all along without requiring retrospective foreshadowing.

Do not make the younger narrator recognise him as dangerous before the narrator actually had reason to know it.

---

## 3. General population — boxing ability becomes apparent

In general population, the same cocky younger inmate who had earlier fought the narrator eventually turned his attention toward Waitane.

The younger inmate appears to have regarded Waitane as an easier target.

Waitane did everything he could to avoid the confrontation.

He attempted to defuse the situation rather than escalate it.

The conflict continued until one evening after dinner during recreation time, when the younger inmate provoked a response and the situation finally came to a head.

---

## 4. The confrontation — established recollection

Once the first punch was thrown, Waitane responded almost immediately.

The author's recollection of the movement is:
- a fast bob manoeuvre;
- a left fake / feint;
- a right hook;
- movement so fast the confrontation was effectively finished before it could properly register.

The author compares the movement to **Mike Tyson** as a visual reference for the compact speed and boxing mechanics, not as a claim that Waitane possessed Tyson's level of ability.

The encounter was over within seconds.

The other inmate suffered a severely bloodied / blown-out nose.

Blood ended up:
- on Waitane;
- across his white prison-issue T-shirt;
- inside the cell.

The physical result exposed what Waitane's ordinary manner had concealed: he was an experienced boxer who could move exceptionally quickly when forced to respond.

---

## 5. Waitane's reaction afterwards

Waitane did **not** react like someone pleased with the outcome.

He panicked.

He did not know what to do next.

This is important to preserve because it is consistent with the person the narrator knew:
- quiet;
- introverted;
- conflict-avoidant;
- not interested in prison status through violence.

The scene should not later be rewritten into a triumphant fighter reveal.

His technical ability and his discomfort with what had just happened belong together.

---

## 6. Immediate protection from disciplinary consequences

The men around Waitane reacted quickly.

They gathered around him so the blood covering his white prison-issue T-shirt could not easily be seen while he was moved back toward his own cell.

Once there:
- Waitane changed out of the blood-covered clothing;
- the clothes were placed into a laundry bag;
- the narrator took the bag;
- the narrator handed it to one of the boys who worked in the prison laundry;
- the purpose was to have the clothing dealt with and keep Waitane out of the disciplinary trouble the visible evidence could otherwise have created.

This aftermath is important character and social material.

The scene is not only about Waitane being able to fight.

It also shows:
- other inmates immediately protecting him;
- the narrator actively participating in that protection;
- practical knowledge of how prison systems and evidence worked;
- the difference between prison violence as spectacle and violence followed by rapid collective damage control.

Do not invent who suggested each step unless later recollection establishes it.

---

## 7. Character contrast

Waitane has strong potential as an incarceration-era character because his traits pull against each other in useful but factual ways.

### What was visible

- extremely quiet;
- introverted;
- unaffiliated;
- reluctant to engage in conflict;
- someone who could plausibly be mistaken for an easy target.

### What was not immediately visible

- featherweight boxer;
- regional champion;
- technically trained;
- extremely fast once forced to respond.

### What the incident reveals

The boxing ability does not replace the quieter person.

It explains why an apparently easy target suddenly became a disastrous choice for the aggressor.

The stronger characterisation is therefore not "secret hard man."

It is a quiet, conflict-avoidant man who happened to possess highly developed fighting skill and had no interest in demonstrating it until he was pushed past the point where avoidance remained possible.

---

## 8. Relationship to the narrator

The narrator became close enough to Waitane during incarceration that the aftermath of the fight was treated as a shared problem rather than something to watch from a distance.

The narrator helped:
- conceal the immediate evidence;
- get Waitane back to his cell;
- remove the blood-covered clothing from the immediate environment;
- route it through a trusted laundry worker.

This suggests the relationship had developed materially beyond the narrator's earliest racial and affiliation-based assumptions.

That implication should be allowed to emerge through chronology and action.

Do not use present-day narration to explain away or morally repair the narrator's earlier beliefs.

The contrast between earlier conduct and later loyalty can stand on its own.

---

## 9. Potential role within FOUR FLIGHTS

Unlike the Perko post-release material, Waitane's boxing incident belongs **inside the incarceration chronology**.

It therefore has genuine potential for future inclusion in FOUR FLIGHTS or another incarceration-era section if the author decides the chapter can carry the additional scene without losing momentum.

Possible narrative function:
1. Waitane and Perko are encountered early.
2. Waitane remains quiet and unobtrusive.
3. General population allows the narrator to know him more fully.
4. The earlier aggressor mistakes that quietness for weakness.
5. Waitane repeatedly attempts to avoid trouble.
6. The recreation-time confrontation reveals his boxing background through action.
7. The immediate aftermath shifts from violence to collective protection and cleanup.
8. The narrator's involvement shows how relationships inside have changed since classification.

This sequence should not be inserted mechanically.

Its value must be judged against FOUR FLIGHTS' current pacing, chronology and existing pressure arcs.

---

## 10. Split-temporal rule

The established project rule applies:

- the reader should learn Waitane's capabilities when the narrator learned them;
- do not foreshadow him as secretly dangerous;
- do not use present-day hindsight to turn him into a symbolic lesson;
- do not make his domestic-assault conviction explain his personality or boxing;
- do not infer motives for the original offence;
- do not treat boxing ability as evidence about the domestic-assault charge.

The memoir can state the conviction as author recollection while keeping it separate from unsupported interpretation.

## 12. Current editorial decision

Preserve this material as a strong incarceration-era character and scene-development record.

Waitane should remain:
- quiet before the reveal;
- technically formidable when forced to respond;
- panicked rather than triumphant afterwards;
- protected by men who had come to know him.

The potential power of the scene comes from the contrast between the man people thought they were looking at and the ability that appeared only when avoidance failed.

No manuscript change is authorised by this record alone.
