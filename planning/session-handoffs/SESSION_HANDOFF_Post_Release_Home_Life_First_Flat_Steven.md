## 4. Established chronology already captured

The following has already been reconstructed and should be treated as established recollection unless the author corrects it.

### Release and arrival at Lynn's

- release occurred in Invercargill;
- day remembered as clear and cold;
- Stacey met narrator at the prison gates;
- narrator had very few possessions;
- narrator received a NZ Government "Steps to Freedom" cheque for approximately $280 after incarceration of six months or more;
- cheque was cashed after release;
- narrator and Stacey went to Lynn's state house;
- Lynn's house was a small, tidy three-bedroom weatherboard state house in a quiet cul-de-sac down a long shared driveway;
- narrator and Stacey had the rear bedroom off the kitchen;
- Melissa and Nakita shared a bedroom off the lounge;
- Lynn had the other bedroom off the lounge;
- house was notably quiet;
- narrator knew Melissa well and regarded her much like a younger sister;
- narrator barely knew Nakita;
- narrator had met Lynn only a few times before;
- Lynn welcomed narrator but there was initial awkwardness;
- Stacey and Melissa living with Lynn was itself an unusual family arrangement because they had not lived with her since infancy.

### First day out

- narrator used part of the $280 to buy half an ounce of cannabis;
- this was cannabis only, not harder drugs;
- narrator and Stacey visited a long-time friend's house that functioned as a natural social gathering point;
- this helped spread word that narrator was out;
- Lynn cooked roast lamb shoulder with vegetables that night;
- homemade moonshine given to narrator as a welcome-home gift was shared;
- Lynn rarely drank;
- awkwardness eased through the evening;
- narrator distinctly remembers the simple pleasure of being out and eating a roast.

### Early routine

- narrator adapted quickly;
- woke early by default;
- made morning runs;
- collected money;
- reloaded;
- reported to parole;
- returned home;
- narrator consciously believed he had learned in prison to narrow criminal activity rather than engage in multiple offence types;
- narrator was not doing burglaries or car theft at this stage;
- this should not be reframed as full rehabilitation.

### Farmers lay-by / plan to establish a home

- narrator and Stacey had already lived together before imprisonment;
- Lynn's house was always intended to be temporary;
- narrator did not want to outstay his welcome or feel like a bludger;
- narrator gave Lynn several hundred dollars;
- narrator quietly put household goods on lay-by at Farmers;
- goods included cookware, towels, blankets, crockery and other basics;
- narrator intended this as groundwork for a future home with Stacey;
- Stacey was not told because narrator wanted it to be a surprise.

### Shane

- Shane was Nakita's father;
- Shane and Lynn had an on-again/off-again relationship;
- narrator knew Shane before moving into Lynn's;
- narrator and Shane had previous run-ins;
- narrator had previously gone after Shane after hearing Shane had assaulted Lynn;
- narrator acknowledges that protection was part of the motive, but that at that age he also used such situations as justification to fight;
- narrator viewed Shane as manipulative, lacking integrity and likely to act for his own benefit;
- narrator nevertheless regarded Lynn's relationship as Lynn's business unless Shane did something to Lynn or the girls.

### Shane returning secretly

- Stacey and Melissa had told Lynn they did not want Shane around;
- Lynn had agreed;
- Shane began staying/visiting secretly;
- narrator unexpectedly returned through the back door one morning and found Lynn and Shane in the kitchen;
- Lynn appeared caught out;
- Shane acted casually;
- narrator exchanged brief hellos and went to his room;
- Stacey was told immediately on waking;
- Melissa was told later;
- within a couple of weeks the secrecy effectively ended;
- Shane began appearing openly;
- Shane made some effort to keep peace with narrator through small talk and offering sessions;
- narrator tolerated his presence while no harm was being done to Lynn or the girls.

### LSD background

- a former jail contact had access to a significant LSD supply in Dunedin;
- Dunedin was approximately 3.5 hours away;
- narrator was initially not able to buy at scale;
- contact fronted narrator approximately 1000 tabs;
- narrator was to return in a month, pay for them, and bring cash for half the next reload;
- this placed narrator in a strong supply position in Southland;
- LSD moved more slowly than some other things narrator had going on and stock accumulated;
- its major significance comes later;
- it eventually helped financially support the later years of culinary school;
- it ultimately funded the move to Auckland for the first job after study;
- none of that future role was known or planned at this stage;
- do not prematurely turn LSD into the central plot of this section.

### New Year gathering

- narrator asked Lynn approximately two weeks before New Year if he could host a small gathering;
- Lynn agreed;
- intended group included narrator, Stacey, Lynn, Melissa, Nakita, Steven/CJ, SI and Shane;
- narrator organised steaks, sausages, charcoal for the Weber, moonshine, chairs in the shed, stereo and barbecue setup;
- SI arrived early;
- SI was a tall, lean Māori man;
- narrator notes SI was unusual within his friendship group because nearly all other close friends were European;
- SI commonly wore worn steel-cap work boots, jeans or cargos, metal-band shirts and the same oversized green bomber jacket regardless of weather;
- the story of how narrator met SI remains deliberately deferred;
- Steven arrived with his best mate Sam;
- Sam was also Stacey and Melissa's cousin;
- younger boys were allowed to drink lightly but had to eat first;
- they were also allowed to smoke cannabis with the older boys;
- this contradiction reflects narrator's internal rules at the time and should not be sanitised.

### Shane's father

- Shane arrived after lunch with his father;
- father was already intoxicated;
- nobody expected or wanted him there;
- there had been serious allegations over the years regarding inappropriate involvement with underage girls;
- **no charges had been laid**;
- do not convert allegations into established fact;
- narrator and SI were both aware of the allegations;
- Lynn appeared uncomfortable with his arrival;
- narrator made it clear he was not welcome;
- explanation was that he would only stay briefly for one drink after dropping Shane off;
- he did not leave.

### Escalation involving Melissa

- by around 20:00 Shane's father was heavily intoxicated;
- it was still light outside;
- Shane and Lynn were moving in and out of the shed;
- Shane's father made a pass at Melissa;
- Melissa appeared uncomfortable and moved her seat;
- narrator noticed immediately;
- narrator loudly checked in with Melissa;
- Melissa moved away and stood with the others;
- narrator warned Shane to get his father moving before he did something stupid and got hurt;
- Shane dismissed him as harmless;
- Shane spoke quietly to his father;
- Shane said he would leave after finishing his drink;
- roughly half an hour later Shane's father appeared to be leaving;
- instead, after urinating in the garden, he returned to his cooler, took another drink, sat beside Melissa and put his hand on her knee;
- narrator reacted immediately;
- narrator shouted "Oi!" to disrupt the behaviour;
- narrator knocked the beer from his hand;
- bottle smashed on the concrete;
- narrator grabbed him by the shirt and physically ejected him;
- narrator told him to "fuck off while you've still got the chance to walk";
- Shane's father landed on his hands and knees and scraped/cut them on the concrete.

### Fight with Shane

- Shane returned because of the commotion;
- Shane advanced on narrator;
- Steven and Sam tried to de-escalate;
- Sam backed off when told;
- Steven tried again by explaining to Shane what had happened and telling him to get his father out;
- Shane followed his father up the driveway;
- father left;
- Shane then returned angry about his father's injuries;
- Shane described him as a harmless old man;
- narrator did not accept that framing;
- narrator states that at that age he did not wait to be struck first once he had decided violence was necessary;
- narrator and Shane fought;
- neighbour came outside;
- Lynn came outside;
- SI tried to intervene;
- narrator describes an internal rule that such conflicts were generally one-on-one;
- Shane accidentally caught SI with a stray swing;
- SI then became directly involved;
- scene became chaotic;
- girls were screaming;
- Lynn called police.

### Police arrival

- Lynn appears to have called police primarily to stop the chaos rather than to have narrator or Shane arrested;
- Stacey immediately understood police involvement could threaten narrator's parole;
- Shane ran up the driveway;
- police arrived while Shane was running toward them;
- officers treated the call as a domestic-assault incident;
- Shane was already known to police for domestic violence at that address;
- it was New Year's Eve and police were busy;
- a second police unit arrived shortly after;
- Shane resisted and gave police difficulty;
- this effectively fixed police attention on Shane;
- Lynn had gone inside;
- narrator made himself scarce;
- police took Shane and left;
- narrator was not arrested;
- police apparently regarded the immediate problem as resolved.

### After police left

- gathering continued;
- narrator and others were familiar enough with chaotic events and police involvement that it did not immediately end the night;
- Lynn did not rejoin them;
- Shane's arrest marked the beginning of the deterioration at Lynn's, not the immediate end;
- Lynn recognised that her call had resulted in the arrest;
- narrator and others also believed a deeper issue was that Melissa's mother should have protected her before narrator needed to intervene;
- Lynn and Shane may not have shared that interpretation;
- do not present narrator's interpretation as objective universal fact.

### Slow deterioration

- Lynn became distant;
- Shane was absent for a period;
- Stacey and Melissa were used to Lynn being inconsistently present;
- things cooled after roughly a week;
- around three weeks later Shane reappeared;
- this occurred around the same period narrator's parole officer changed;
- Stacey and Melissa were angry with Lynn;
- arguments became frequent;
- Shane avoided narrator;
- narrator and Stacey had nowhere else to go yet.

### Petty incidents and dog disappearance

- shed began being locked at night even though narrator says this had not previously been normal;
- narrator's bike was inside;
- this forced him to walk, including across town to parole reporting;
- narrator could not prove deliberate intent;
- narrator's dog disappeared three times;
- first time narrator never understood how she got off the chain, but she was found near a service station;
- second time she had been taken by another household in the cul-de-sac;
- that household and the associated Melissa incident are deferred to another reconstruction;
- third time the dog disappeared and was never seen again;
- Melissa told narrator she had overheard Shane say he was going to kill the dog;
- narrator trusted Melissa and saw no reason she would lie;
- narrator **could not prove Shane caused the dog's disappearance**;
- preserve that uncertainty;
- the third disappearance was the point narrator decided it was time to leave.

### Ma and the rental search

- narrator and Stacey did not care much about possessions;
- this would be narrator's first independent rental;
- narrator had usually rented rooms in other people's houses;
- narrator had also previously lived with SI in a house left to SI and his siblings after SI's father's death;
- narrator needed Ma's help with transport and expected he might need her as guarantor;
- Ma knew Shane and his family because they lived across the road from her in Tuatapere;
- narrator and Ma had a turbulent history;
- despite financial instability after Dad's death, Ma was resourceful, careful with money and had very good credit;
- narrator had to convince her the situation was serious enough and that he was making a real effort;
- narrator told her he was not doing anything dodgy;
- narrator believes Ma probably knew this was not completely true;
- Ma could nevertheless see narrator was making a more stable effort and was less reckless.

### Culinary school as practical credibility

At this point in the chronology:

- narrator still did not care about culinary school itself;
- attendance was useful because it kept parole satisfied;
- attendance also gave Ma evidence that narrator was doing something constructive;
- narrator admits he overstated his enthusiasm for the course to Ma;
- narrator's continued attendance must **not** yet be rewritten as passion, calling, talent recognition or commitment to becoming a chef;
- it was still primarily instrumental.

### First flat

- budget was extremely low;
- approximately $180 per week was workable in Southland at the time;
- criteria were basic: clean, dry, decent area, affordable;
- narrator and Stacey searched for around three days;
- most places were unsuitable;
- they found a small single-storey flat in a block of three;
- communal laundry was at the top of the driveway;
- an older couple occupied the first flat;
- middle flat was also available;
- narrator preferred the end flat for privacy and security;
- Stacey viewed it with narrator;
- flat was very small;
- two bedrooms: one double and one single;
- front door opened into kitchen;
- lounge was open plan;
- bedrooms came off the main living area;
- flat was clean, on a good street, affordable, at the end of the drive and close to Polytech;
- decision was effectively immediate;
- application was easy;
- Ma did not ultimately need to sign as guarantor;
- move-in requirement was approximately two weeks' bond, two weeks in advance and approximately another two weeks' equivalent to move in;
- total was just under $1000;
- Ma fronted the cost;
- keys were handed over immediately.

### Moving in

- narrator and Stacey had almost nothing;
- their actual possessions were essentially a mattress, television and clothes;
- those items were still at Lynn's when they got the keys;
- narrator convinced Ma to let him tie the mattress to the roof of her car;
- narrator remembers this as harder than convincing her to fund the move-in costs;
- Ma realised how little they had;
- narrator mentioned the Farmers lay-by and only asked whether Ma might pay off the towels;
- Ma paid the entire lay-by;
- this gave them cookware, crockery, towels, blankets and other household basics;
- Ma then drove roughly an hour back into the country;
- narrator and Stacey were left alone in their first flat;
- narrator notes there is no meaningful PG version of their first night;
- current capture only needs to preserve that they immediately "christened" the place;
- explicit sexual detail is unnecessary unless later relevant.

### Stacey and the meaning of home

- narrator was already aware at the time that Stacey did not care about possessions or material status;
- narrator had moved frequently enough that home was simply where he laid his head;
- Stacey was content simply being with narrator;
- narrator recognised this then but could not understand it;
- narrator still finds it difficult to fully understand;
- narrator considers himself to have been mature for his age but shaped by a very different social world;
- this uncomplicated attachment contributed to the intensity of their relationship;
- narrator and Stacey have now been separated for roughly as long as they were together;
- narrator has never forgotten this aspect of her.

### Life in the flat

- day-to-day routine changed very little;
- narrator still had parole, Polytech and the same means of making money;
- major difference was control over his environment;
- narrator could now have selected associates come to his own home;
- narrator remained very private and security-conscious;
- only a small number of people were allowed access.

### Steven / court

- narrator returned to court, but not because of his own offending;
- Steven had already spent time in a boys' home;
- Steven was at risk of being returned to care;
- narrator intervened and offered to have Steven live with him;
- narrator offered to take responsibility for him;
- the judge happened to be the same judge who had previously sentenced narrator;
- judge found the situation somewhat amusing/ironic;
- narrator had evidence of parole adherence, culinary-school attendance, fixed abode and independent tenancy;
- judge preferred family placement over institutional care where possible;
- judge made clear narrator would be legally responsible for Steven;
- if Steven returned to court, narrator could expect to be there too;
- judge had reservations but agreed to give them the opportunity;
- the judge's warning is remembered in substance as giving them enough rope and leaving it to them whether they used it well or hung themselves with it;
- do not convert this into exact dialogue unless narrator later confirms wording.

### Narrator and Steven

- narrator and Steven were extremely close despite periods apart;
- Steven had been in care before narrator went to prison;
- shortly before narrator's incarceration, around narrator's birthday, narrator, SI and Stacey took Steven away camping for a weekend without approval;
- they told Steven to meet them in Invercargill during lunchtime while he was at school;
- they left with him knowing permission would never have been granted;
- narrator describes this jokingly as "kidnapped, for lack of a better term";
- this was not provided as a precise legal characterisation;
- once Steven later moved into the flat officially, the relationship changed because narrator now had direct responsibility for him;
- narrator set rules and boundaries;
- Steven was expected to assimilate into the household.
