## 2. INTERVIEW PROCESS

### 2.1 Critical Gap Identified

The Jay confrontation was **introduced but not interviewed**. Previous session covered Jay's background but was interrupted to draft existing material. The actual incident remains unexplored.

### 2.2 Interview Questions Required

**The Incident Setup:**
- How did it start? A look, a word, resources, territory?
- Where were you when it happened — yard, wing, dining hall?
- Was it sudden or did tension build?
- Did you know who he was at that point, or find out after?

**The Confrontation:**
- What was said? Who spoke first?
- Was it just you and Jay, or were others involved/watching?
- Did it escalate to physical, or was it contained?
- If physical — what happened? Duration, outcome, injuries?
- If not physical — what stopped it? The kingpin connection?

**The Aftermath:**
- How did it resolve? Sorted the prison way?
- Did your relationship with Jay change after?
- Was this the "life-threatening" moment, or was that separate?
- Did the kingpin connection come into play here?

**The Second Incident:**
- You mentioned "one that might have been life-threatening if carried out" — is this Jay or someone else?
- What happened?

**Additional Material Needed:**
- Stacey's visits — Sunday ritual, walking to prison, what those hours meant
- Wing cleaner role with Ivian — running laps, talking shit, the dynamics
- Kitchen work — the ironic foreshadowing
- Parole board knockback details — the woman, the file, "Every. Single. One."

### 2.3 Interview Approach

Per memoir-interview skill methodology:
1. Open with sensory anchors — where were you standing, what did you hear?
2. Let details emerge naturally, don't force chronology
3. Follow emotional threads, not just facts
4. Capture dialogue fragments exactly as remembered
5. Note body language, physical sensations, environmental texture

## 3. DRAFT BASE CONTENT

### 3.1 Existing Material to Integrate

From 03_FreshForUnlock.md (approved v0.3):
```
There was a guy named Jay.

About five foot tall. Built like a fucking bulldozer—the kind of compact muscle 
that comes from years of violence and nothing else to do.

I had no idea who he was on the outside. Just seemed like a cocky fuckwit, 
really. Māori fella. Moved through the wing like he owned it.

I would later find out the story.

Jay had wanted to join Mongrel Mob—one of the most feared gangs in New Zealand. 
Prospected for them. Did what prospects do.

Then decided they were weak.

Not hard enough for what he'd built them up to be in his head. So he started his 
own gang instead.

Let that sink in.

This was a man who looked at Mongrel Mob and said *I can do better*.

Not someone to cross paths with in the wrong way.

But that's exactly what I did.
```

### 3.2 Draft Structure (Post-Interview)

1. **Atmospheric Opening** — The moment before it starts. Where you are. What you're doing. The ordinary becoming dangerous.

2. **The Incident** — What happened. Dialogue. Action. The economy of violence or near-violence.

3. **The Physics of It** — If physical: the fight itself. Duration, blows, outcome. If not: what contained it.

4. **Resolution** — How it ended. The prison logic of "sorted."

5. **Aftermath** — Did anything change? The kingpin card if played.

6. **Bridge** — Other moments. Stacey's visits. The parole board. Transition toward release/next section.

### 3.3 Voice Adherence Checklist (Pre-Draft)

- [ ] First-person, past-tense dominant
- [ ] Blunt, confident, profane with intent
- [ ] Short staccato lines for impact
- [ ] Emotion shown through action, not declared
- [ ] No forced epiphanies
- [ ] Sensory immersion (sounds, smells, physical space)
- [ ] Authentic terminology
- [ ] Dark humour as survival mechanism

## 6. MAKE REQUIRED ADJUSTMENTS

### 6.1 Revision Triggers

Watch for:
- "That's not quite how it happened" — Historical accuracy issue
- "I wouldn't say it like that" — Voice consistency issue
- "Too soft" / "Too dramatic" — Tonal calibration needed
- "The timeline is wrong" — Structural issue

### 6.2 Revision Process

1. **Document specific feedback** — Line-by-line if needed
2. **Categorise issues** — Voice, accuracy, structure, rhythm
3. **Prioritise fixes** — Historical accuracy first, then voice, then polish
4. **Revise draft** — Targeted edits, not full rewrites
5. **Re-verify** against voice profile
6. **Resubmit** for approval

### 6.3 Version Control

- v0.1 — Initial draft post-interview
- v0.2 — Post first review adjustments
- v0.3 — Final polish (if needed)
- v1.0 — Approved for manuscript
