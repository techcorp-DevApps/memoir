# YOU'RE ONLY AS GOOD AS YOUR LAST SERVICE

This first one isn't about cooking.

It's about time.

---

There's this thing that happens when you're young and invincible.

You think there's always more.

More hours. More days. More chances to say the thing you should've said, do the
thing you kept putting off, be present in the moment you let slip past because you
were tired, or busy, or just not paying attention.

You tell yourself: tomorrow.

Tomorrow I'll call. Tomorrow I'll visit. Tomorrow I'll sit down and actually be
there instead of half-listening while my brain runs through tomorrow's prep list or
next week's problems or whatever bullshit feels urgent in the moment.

Tomorrow. Tomorrow. Tomorrow.

And then one day, there is no tomorrow.

Not for that person. Not for that conversation. Not for that version of your life
where you still had the chance to choose differently.

---

I'm not going to tell you some long story here.

I'm not going to dress it up in kitchen metaphors or make it clever.

Some lessons don't come with a funny anecdote and a punchline. They just
arrive—unannounced, uninvited—and rearrange everything you thought you knew about
what matters.

This is one of those.

---

## ChefTip #1

**Make time for the people who matter. Not tomorrow. Now.**

Not when the service is over. Not when the season slows down. Not when you've hit
that next milestone or ticked that next box or finally feel like you've earned the
right to breathe.

Now.

Because life doesn't wait for you to be ready.

I've learned a lot of things over the years.

How to break down a fish. How to balance a sauce. How to survive a Saturday night
that should've killed us all.

But the most important thing I've ever learned can't be taught in a kitchen.

It's this:

**Tomorrow is not guaranteed.**

The most important role you will ever have—the most important position—is you.

Make time for your family. Your friends.

Give them your best service.

Make the moment matter.

Here's the thing about family, though.

Sometimes it's not the one you're born into.

Sometimes it's the people standing next to you when the rail is full and the
printer won't stop screaming and your feet stopped existing two hours ago. The ones
who call you a cunt at 7:15 and cover your section at 7:16 without being asked.

Sometimes family is forged in heat.

Literally.
