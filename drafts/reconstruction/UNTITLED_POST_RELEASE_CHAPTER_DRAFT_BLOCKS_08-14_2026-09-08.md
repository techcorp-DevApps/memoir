## BLOCK 8 — The Warning

**STATUS: RESTRUCTURED DRAFT FOR AUTHOR ASSESSMENT — NOT APPROVED**

By around eight that evening, Shane’s father was heavily intoxicated. It was still light outside.

Shane and Lynn were moving in and out of the shed when his father made a pass at Melissa. She didn’t make a scene. She ignored him, shifted her chair further away and tried to carry on, but I saw the movement and knew something was wrong.

I asked if she was all good, loudly enough that everybody heard. She didn’t really answer. She got up instead and moved across to stand with the boys and talk with Stace.

The next time Shane came into the shed, I told him to get his old man moving before he did something stupid and got hurt. Shane waved it away, then leaned over and said something quietly to his father.

He assured me the old man would leave when he finished his drink.

## BLOCK 9 — The Fight

**STATUS: RESTRUCTURED DRAFT FOR AUTHOR ASSESSMENT — NOT APPROVED**

About half an hour later, the old man stood up and walked out toward the garden to take a piss. I thought that was it.

Then he came back, opened his cooler and helped himself to another drink. With all the fucking cheek in the world, he sat down beside Melissa and put his hand on her knee.

“Oi!”

I knocked the beer from his hand. The bottle smashed on the concrete and I had him by the scruff of his shirt before he could properly react.

“Fuck off while you’ve still got the chance to walk.”

I threw him out of the shed. He landed on his hands and knees, scraping and cutting them on the concrete.

The commotion brought Shane back. He came toward me, but Steven and Sam stepped in before it went any further. I told the younger boys to sit down. Sam did. Steven stayed long enough to tell Shane what his father had done and convince him to get the old man out of there.

For a moment, that worked. Shane followed his father up the driveway, the old man got into his vehicle and left, and it looked like the whole thing might be over.

Then Shane came back.

“He’s just a harmless old man.”

Apparently the way his father had been put out mattered more to him than the reason for it.

At that age, once I decided violence had become necessary, I didn’t wait for the other person to throw the first punch.

Shane and I went toe to toe. The neighbour came outside. Lynn came out. Si tried to get between us before either of us did serious damage, then Shane caught him with a stray punch.

Wrong bloke. Wrong moment.

Si was in it now too.

## BLOCK 10 — The Police

**STATUS: RESTRUCTURED DRAFT FOR AUTHOR ASSESSMENT — NOT APPROVED**

The girls were screaming and Lynn was shouting that she had called the police. Stace understood what that meant immediately. An arrest for me didn’t just mean an arrest. I was on parole.

Shane broke away and ran up the driveway just as the first police car turned in. The officers knew both him and the address from earlier domestic incidents, so their attention went straight to him. When he resisted, a second unit arrived.

Lynn disappeared back inside. I made myself scarce. The police took Shane and left.

It was New Year’s Eve, and they had plenty more calls waiting.

────────

## BLOCK 11 — What Changed

**STATUS: RESTRUCTURED DRAFT FOR AUTHOR ASSESSMENT — NOT APPROVED**

We didn’t see Lynn again for the rest of the night.

The music went back on. The drinks came out again. Whatever was left on the barbecue got eaten. The fight hadn’t ended the gathering, but it had changed the house.

Lynn knew her phone call had resulted in Shane being arrested, and it seemed to sit badly with her. From where most of us stood, the arrest wasn’t the only issue. Melissa was her daughter. Someone should have protected her before I ever needed to intervene.

Lynn would probably have seen it differently. So would Shane.

Afterward, she became distant and Shane disappeared for a while. Stace and Melissa weren’t particularly surprised by their mother withdrawing. From what I had seen—and from how the girls understood their relationship with her—Lynn had never been consistently present for them in the same way she had been for Nakita.

After about a week, the open hostility subsided. Nothing had been resolved. People had simply stopped saying it out loud.

Parole, by comparison, had remained one of the least complicated parts of being out.

Then he retired.
