# Return to Yulara

*Working title. No chapter number assigned.*

---

I went back to Yulara.

That was the job. And this time they weren't asking me to run one kitchen — they
wanted all of it. Every outlet in the hotel. Commissary, pastry, bakery. Five menus
to rewrite, three new venues to open, sixty chefs on the floor and about a hundred
on the roster.

And one of those new venues was going to be mine. High-end. Molecular.
Amuse-bouche changing every day.

Exactly the sort of thing I'd been waiting for.

So, obviously, I said yes.

---

Brett was running the kitchen when I arrived. First few days were straightforward
enough — plenty of familiar faces still around, which made walking back in easier.

Then Brett asked me to go down to the bistro.

He'd been covering the pass himself. That wasn't a particularly encouraging sign.

Head chef gone. Sous gone. Lewis running the place, Brazilian bloke. And a fair
chunk of my old crew still standing there.

I walked towards the pass and they saw me. You could see the reaction. They were
pleased to see me. They were also fucking terrified.

Brett introduced me to Lewis and his face dropped. Fair enough. He'd probably heard
the stories, and now he's got the bloke who wrote the menu standing behind him while
he's trying to run it.

I felt for him. So I didn't stay. Nothing to be gained from watching him cook a
service with me breathing down his neck.

Let him get on with it.

---

My days were meant to be spent in the main kitchen. Menus, financials, suppliers,
samples. Purchasing, planning, refurbishment, refit.

Then Brett came back. The bistro had a couple of tour groups on top of normal
service. Could I give them a hand?

I'd never had anyone expedite for me. I'd done service with less staff than they
had, so I figured it couldn't be that bad.

Two groups. Fifty or sixty each. Set menu, at-table self-serve.

I went in through the kitchen side and started looking around. Fridges first.

> "Are we ready for service?"

> "Where's your function mise?"

Opened the next fridge.

Filthy. Shit everywhere.

Right.

Service started. Lewis running his side, me expediting, the team clunky as hell.

Then I realised. This wasn't about giving them a hand. Brett wanted me there because
the place needed somebody who could actually run the service.

Then Brett fucking bails.

Lol.

---

I went back early that afternoon and had a yarn with Lewis. Where were the problems,
what was he struggling with, what did he need, what could we fix immediately. We
went through it.

Then dinner came around and it was still hard. It shouldn't have been. But it was.

So we did what you do. Get through service, work out what went wrong, come back
tomorrow.

Except tomorrow there was something else.

---

Day six. Eight in the morning, main kitchen, helping the canteen chef before getting
into my own paperwork.

About half ten, Brett walked over. Stopped. Looked at me.

> "You busy, mate?"

> "Always busy, Chef. What ya need done?"

> "Do me a favour. Go down the road and make sure they're ready for service. Lewis
> hasn't shown up. Can't get hold of him. Security's on the way for a welfare
> check."

Right.

Middle of the desert. If somebody was rostered on and nobody could get hold of them,
security went to their accommodation. If nobody answered, they went in. Sometimes
people had died out there. More often they'd just fucked off.

This time, that's what had happened.

*Of course.*

I knew what was coming. I was running the bistro again, full time, on top of
everything else.

Not fucking impressed.

But it is what it is. How hard can it be, right?

---

I got the new menus finished. That part was easy enough.

Wasn't particularly happy about it, mind. There's something slightly irritating
about being brought back to fix something you already fixed once.

Still. Job's a job.

What I was waiting for was somebody to take the bistro off me so I could get started
on the flagship. That was what I'd actually come back for.

Then they opened a noodle bar next door.

Mine. Apparently.

---

The chef running it was Cantonese and didn't speak a word of English, so I inherited
his brother as translator. His brother was a gay hairdresser who'd never worked a
day in a kitchen.

Which was probably not the strangest staffing decision I'd seen by that point.

The chef was Hebe. Barely five foot, coke-bottle glasses.

Fuck, he could cook.

Proper Cantonese technique — that was the good bit. I taught him some of the modern
prep I was using and he taught me Cantonese cooking, and there was plenty I didn't
know. That was the part I liked. You can be the bloke in the room who knows exactly
what he's doing and still have somebody walk in and show you something completely
different.

The noodle bar went off. Line out into the town square. Hebe was loving it.

And for once, somebody else was getting the headaches. At least for that kitchen.

---

Eventually I got onto the flagship, and now I was where I wanted to be.

Everything I'd picked up over the years was going onto one menu. Fat Sam. Chef. Kein
Do. All the kitchens before them. And the chefs I was obsessed with at the time —
Thomas Keller, Grant Achatz, the Adrià brothers. I was reading everything, studying
everything, trying to work out how far I could push it.

So Keller's tomato entrée went on. Confit tomato, tomato sorbet you could read
through, crispy Parmesan wafer, heirloom tomatoes — every one peeled by hand.

Which sounds impressive until you're the commis doing the peeling.

The signature was a trio of black onyx beef. Short rib, eye fillet, tongue. Parmesan
gnocchi, fava bean, turnip, sauce soubise. The amuse was my version of Alinea's
banana and duck curry, changing every day.

*[More dishes on record as offered, not yet collected — interview gap, not a
decision. Flagged below, not filled.]*

This was the restaurant I'd wanted. So I was into it. Properly into it. And I wanted
everything right.

It had my name on it.

---

Jackson was born in the middle of all this. Alice Springs was the nearest city.

I was opening a restaurant.

That was life.

---

The tasting was supposed to be five people. GM, F&B director, restaurant manager,
exec chef, exec sous.

Five. Easy.

Except every chef in the main kitchen who could get away from their station turned
up. The ones who'd worked under me before took the front row.

That was nice. And slightly terrifying.

I had two juniors running plates, brother and sister. She was a CDP off Philippe
Michelle, he was a commis out of Number 8. They weren't cooking, they were running —
two of everything, out to the executives, back to the kitchen, next dish.

They were fucking proud of themselves. I was proud of them too.

Then came the questions.

And for the first time since I'd been a junior, I had that feeling again. Someone
watching. Not because I didn't know the food — I knew the food, I'd built the menu,
I'd cooked it. But everyone in that room was listening.

So you answer. You explain. You stand behind what you've done.

And you don't fucking blink.

---

The tasting went well. Very well. Not one negative word.

Inside, I was waiting for somebody to find something. Outside, I was confident.
That's what the team needed.

I'd built a reputation by then. Bit of a pit bull. Fair, though — always fair. If
something wasn't right I'd say so, and if somebody questioned something I expected
them to have a reason.

The GM was German. I never knew her name. I called her Ma'am.

She raised one issue. One.

Not the food. Not the taste. Not the cook. Not the overall presentation.

A garnish.

One garnish.

The kitchen went quiet. I asked her to explain.

She explained. It made even less sense.

---

Brett stepped in.

> "Nothing's decided. We'll talk about it later."

No. I wanted to know now.

I'll take criticism from anyone, always have — you can learn something from anybody.
But there has to be something to learn. This wasn't criticism. It was noise. And we
were standing in the kitchen. It was a tasting.

So I pushed.

Brett knew exactly where that conversation could go if it kept going, and he shut it
down.

For reasons I'd find out later.
