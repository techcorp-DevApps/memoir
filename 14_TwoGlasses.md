Something shifted after that.

Not dramatically. Not in any way you'd notice if you weren't paying attention. Sam still rode me. Still appeared at my shoulder like some kind of culinary ghost every time my timing slipped. Still called me every name under the sun when my brunoise looked more like rubble than dice.

Never asked. Never logged it. Just walked behind the bar, selected something with the kind of reverence you'd usually reserve for religious artefacts, and poured two glasses.

One for him. One for me.

At first, I was convinced this was just his excuse to polish off a bottle without drawing unwanted attention. Drinking alone looks like a problem. Drinking with a colleague looks like mentoring.

And honestly? The wine was vile.

Every sip tasted like someone had fermented a sock and called it complexity. Sam would swirl his glass, mutter something about "bramble fruit" or "tertiary notes," and I'd nod like I had any fucking idea what he was talking about. The so-called flavour profiles he spoke of were non-existent.

That was it.

No "well done." No "you've come a long way." Just an acknowledgement that something I made was good enough that we'd need more of it.

From him, that was everything.
