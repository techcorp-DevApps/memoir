## 3. Missing material worth recovering

### 3.1 I CAN COOK BETTER THAN YOU — the whole piece

Nothing of it is in the manuscript. Specific losses:

- **The ChefTip origin story.** *"I've decided to add a segment to my blog called
  ChefTips."* The device has a real origin — a recurring irritation at people
  claiming they can do the job. `08_EarningTheRight.md` currently introduces
  ChefTips with an invented framing (*"Over the years, I've collected lessons…"*)
  that is weaker and less true.
- **The $58 steak rant** — the Mercedes E350 / *"of course you may fuck my wife"*
  escalation. Sustained, funny, and in register.
- **The KitchenAid / Donna Hay passage** — the home cook's stakes against his.
- **Seven pans, three bipolar sauces, the kitchen hand wiping plates with a tea
  towel, table seven half gluten-free and not on the docket, *front of house are
  cunts*.**
- **The bullet list of differences.** The strongest concrete material in the piece:
  - chops without looking
  - laughs at recipes
  - **picks up confit duck bare-handed; lost all feeling years ago; the sensation
    in his index finger "is left with the skin that is now attached to the grill of
    my first job. There it shall remain."**
  - **"We don't have Band-Aids; we have masking tape."**
  - scars and calluses where his palms were
  - *"I don't have an eight o'clock reservation for two… I have a second sitting, a
    headache, and I'm running out of leeks."*
- **The neurosurgeon / Operation line**, and the MasterChef close.
- **The porn analogy** and the Julia Child quote.

**The index-finger detail is the single best unused image in the whole source.**
It is a physical, permanent, cheap-to-place fact that connects the present-day
narrator to his first kitchen. Nothing in 21 chapters does that work.

### 3.2 THE PEOPLE WHO LAUGH WHILE YOU'RE DROWNING — the whole piece

Also entirely absent. Specific losses:

- **The chorus.** *"BEHIND." "HOT." "YES CHEF." "NO, THE OTHER PAN—USE YOUR FUCKING
  EYES."* → *"To anyone else, it sounds like aggression. To us, it sounds like
  breathing."*
- **The near-miss.** The allergy docket — no alliums, no butter, separate pan — and
  reaching for the wrong pan for half a heartbeat, catching it before anything
  irreversible. The guest never knows.
- **Chef talking him back onto the rails**: *"Look at me… Call your times."* /
  *"Good. Again."*
- **The brigade closing around him without a word about it**: Grill buying a minute
  with *"Beef is plus one, Chef,"* like it was his own call. Fish sliding a pan onto
  the free burner with the forgotten component, no lecture, just a tap on the bench.
  Pastry sending something sweet down with *"Eat or I'll murder you."*
- **The alley afterwards**: *"Remember when apprentice almost baptised the allergy
  plate in jus tonight?"* — then Grill's *"Didn't happen though, did it? That's the
  point."* and Chef's *"You caught it before I had to. That's the job."*
- **The pack passage** — the dishie nobody outside the line gets to talk down to,
  and *"You might be 'an idiot' at 7:05… but at 7:06, if a waiter says something
  sideways, you're: 'My idiot, actually.'"*

`14_TheOnesWhoStay.md` covers adjacent ground — kitchen humour, loyalty as action —
but it does **not** contain any of the above. The two pieces are complementary, not
duplicates. This one is built around a scene; the other around reflection.

## 4. Divergences inside chapters that DID migrate

### 4.1 Sam changed sex

**Source** (in *The Ones Who Stay*):

> *"There was this one sous chef at a place I worked — a woman named Sam who had
> been in the industry since she was fifteen and had the thousand-yard stare to
> prove it."* … *"But I'd take a bullet for that woman."*

**Chapter** `14_TheOnesWhoStay.md`: the same scene, same dialogue — *"Go smoke.
Five minutes. I've got this."* — attributed to **Fat Sam**, male, the vineyard sous.

During restructuring a **female sous chef named Sam was collapsed into Fat Sam.**
Lost with her: *in the industry since she was fifteen*, *the thousand-yard stare*,
and the six months of being frightened of her.

**RESOLVED 2026-08-30 — B25 = B, and the direction matters.**

> *"Sam originally based on the real person, but when routed into [the memoir] it
> was decided that it was imperative to be the real identity — FAT SAM. After all,
> a full chapter is named after him."*

**The blog was the disguise. The memoir is the true version.** The woman-named-Sam
was an anonymised rendering of the real Fat Sam. Restoring his real identity was a
deliberate decision, taken because a whole chapter carries his name.

Nothing to restore. `14_TheOnesWhoStay.md` is correct as written.

### 4.1a The consequence — the source is NOT the more authoritative document

This inverts the assumption running through the rest of this assessment.

A recovered source usually outranks a later edit. **Here it does not.** The blog
was written for a public audience with identities disguised; the memoir was written
to name people truthfully. Where the two disagree about **who someone is**, the
manuscript is right and the source is the softened one.

**So every person in the recovered material is now suspect until Bosco confirms
them** — not their words or actions, which are his and are real, but their names,
sexes and identifying details. That applies to §19 and §20 as much as to Sam.

Named or identified in the recovered pieces, unverified against the real people:
**Sam** (resolved — is Fat Sam) · **Marco** (resolved — composite, Geoffrey's story)
· the unnamed **Chef** in §20 · **Grill**, **Fish**, **Pastry**, the **dishie** ·
the mother, the father, *"my boy"* in §19.

Content survives the disguise. Identity does not. → feeds **B26**

`08_EarningTheRight.md` closes: *"Over the years, I've collected lessons… I call
them ChefTips. They're numbered but not by when I learned them. By how much they
cost… So here they are. Ten ChefTips. I'll even give you one for free."*

The source's real introduction is the blog framing in *I Can Cook Better Than You*.

*If You Didn't Taste It* ("My list was simple on paper"). Both chapters render them
as prose. **Content preserved, no loss.** Recorded only so a future comparison
against the source does not read it as damage.

### The punchline does not survive a find-and-replace

The blog's §19 turns on:

> *"My Dad can't do that. Do you know WHY he can't do that? Because he's a fucking
> dental surgeon, not a chef."*

The gag is the class distance — a surgeon's hands against a chef's. **"He's a
machinist, not a chef" is not the same joke**; the contrast collapses because both
are trades, both are hands, both are shift work.

There is a better joke in the true version and it is not mine to write: a father
who worked a mill and made cabinets has hands with their own damage, which sits
directly against the index-finger detail four paragraphs later. **Flagged, not
drafted.** → B27, when §19 is placed.
