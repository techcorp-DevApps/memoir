⚠ VOICE INCONSISTENCIES REQUIRING CORRECTION
1. Redundant Jay introduction (Lines 23–27)
Chapter 03 ends with a full setup of Jay — his Mongrel Mob history, his physical description, the "I can do better" line. Chapter 04 then repeats nearly identical material:

"I didn't know his story yet. Didn't know he'd prospected for Mongrel Mob, decided they were too weak for him, and started his own gang instead. Didn't know he was the kind of man who looked at one of the most feared gangs in New Zealand and said I can do better."

Problem: A reader moving cohesively through chapters has just read this information. The repetition breaks immersion and feels like scaffolding that wasn't removed.
Fix: Cut lines 23–27 entirely. The reader already knows. Let the scene play.

2. Over-explanation of stakes (Lines 167–171)

"My connection to Joel was what had kept Jay from getting the green light. If I'd been just some muppet — some cocky fuckwit, not a white boy, not a friend of Joel's, not a friend of Ivian's — I'd have been dead in less than a minute."

Problem: This is the narrator explaining what the scene has already shown. The reader doesn't need this spelled out — the Joel conversation made the stakes clear. This paragraph tells us what to feel rather than trusting us to feel it.
Fix: Trim to something more characteristic — perhaps: "My connection to Joel was the only reason I was still walking." Let the gravity land through restraint.

3. Stacey letter description (Lines 212–215)

"And twice a week, a letter would arrive — always a week behind due to processing. The first part would be a generic update of the first or second half of the week, depending on which letter it was."

Problem: This is slightly expository and stilted compared to the rest of the voice. "Depending on which letter it was" is the kind of clarifying detail that feels like explaining rather than immersing.
Fix: Tighten: "Twice a week, a letter would arrive — always a week behind due to processing. The first half was updates. The second half was filthy."

4. "Not fear of the fight. Fear of the reality." (Line 169)
Problem: This is close to working but slightly telegraphs the insight. The established voice prefers to render the moment rather than name the distinction.
Suggested revision: Let the details carry the weight — the walk, the stairs, the awareness that hierarchy saved him. The distinction between physical fear and systemic fear can remain implicit.
